        Note on the math: Variable costs (platform fee, service fee, cedolare secca) are exactly the portion of gross rental income that never reaches you. So the grand total already "contains" that cost, and the correct offset to subtract is the full gross rental income — subtracting only the net amount would double-count the variable costs. The result is mathematically identical to simply offsetting fixed+capital costs with net rental income; we show gross here because the grand total above is the literal fixed+variable+capital sum you asked to see.

        Assumptions & limitations
        • All house-side amounts are in EUR; your budget is in CHF — converted using the exchange rate above, held constant (no currency-risk modeling).
        • Fixed-cost and IMU figures are editable placeholders until phase 2 wires in a real per-comune dataset.
        • Cedolare secca is shown here as a flat rate; the legally correct 1st/2nd/3rd+ property tiering (and the business-activity presumption for a 3rd+ property) is implemented in Module 3.
        • Capital costs use only year-1 mortgage interest, year-1 linear amortization, and year-1 linear family-loan repayment — a snapshot, not a multi-year schedule. The 30-year path lives in Module 2.
        • Opportunity cost of cash equity = cash equity × the expected market return rate above. This is what makes "capital costs" reflect the true cost of tying up that money, not just financing costs.
        • Verdict thresholds (editable in spirit, not yet in the UI): comfortably affordable if net annual cost ≤ budget; tight if ≤ 115% of budget; not affordable above that.
        • This is not tax or legal advice — see the full disclaimers ported from the spreadsheet's "Anleitung" sheet, which will be expanded here in phase 7.
